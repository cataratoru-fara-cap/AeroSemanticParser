  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=1cm]
    \node[fill=gmTint, rounded corners=2mm, inner sep=2.8mm, text width=13.6cm, align=left] {
      \gmwhy{ScrapingAnt, at its cheapest.}\enspace\normalsize Monthly credits: the page as the server
      sends it costs \textbf{1}, rendered in a browser \textbf{10}. KYM's server writes the whole page.};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \note{
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Layman version: we look Doge up in the site's own index, download the page once, and keep
      a copy, the way a library keeps a copy of a book.
    \item The index: Know Your Meme's sitemap lists every page with the date it last changed ---
      9 July 2026 for Doge, at the address KYM gives him today (next stop). Discovery (kym\_discovery) reads it every month and files each address
      in our address book (Mongo, collection urls) with its section --- memes, people, events --- and
      whether KYM's editors confirmed it.
    \item The fetch (kym\_scrape) goes through ScrapingAnt, a scraping service that gets past the
      site's bot checks. It is a subscription with monthly credits; the plain HTML call costs 1
      credit per page, a browser-rendered one 10. KYM is rendered on its server: the plain HTML
      already holds every paragraph, link and image address, and what a browser would add is ads
      and analytics. So: 1 credit, about 40k credits for the whole corpus; failed calls are not
      billed.
    \item We fetched Doge on 9 September: \NDogeRawKB\ KB of HTML (menus and ad slots included, not
      run), kept compressed (\NDogeStoredKB\ KB) with a SHA-256 fingerprint, so a page that did not change is never re-read.
    \item Why keep the page at all: to re-read it later with a better parser, without asking the
      site --- or paying --- again.
  \end{itemize}}
\end{frame}

        \node[ic] at (0,0) {\faCoins};    \node[tx] at (.6,0) {\textbf{Never pay twice}\\[-.4mm]{\small\color{gmInk2}each batch re-checks what is stored}};
        \node[ic] at (0,1) {\faShield*};  \node[tx] at (.6,1) {\textbf{Never lose a good copy}\\[-.4mm]{\small\color{gmInk2}a failed re-fetch keeps the old one}};
        \node[ic] at (0,2) {\faRedo};     \node[tx] at (.6,2) {\textbf{Run it again}\\[-.4mm]{\small\color{gmInk2}a crawl can stop half-way and resume}};
      \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{columns}
  \note{
  \begin{itemize}
    \item 39,972 addresses discovered: the sitemaps first (cheap, and they say when a page
      changed), then KYM's listing pages for what the sitemaps miss. 25,058 are confirmed entries;
      the others are submissions the editors have not confirmed --- we keep their addresses, we do
      not fetch them.
    \item 25,016 pages stored, 6.86 GB of HTML, 1.41 GB on disk. 42 failures: 34 for good (gone,
      forbidden), 8 to retry. \NEntries\ entries parsed; \NParseFailures\ pages are photo galleries,
      videos and articles living under the same addresses (next chapter), and \FDupRetired\ were an
      entry's second address (the previous stop).
    \item Never pay twice: work is split into chunks, and a chunk re-checks Mongo before fetching,
      so if Airflow retries it, pages already bought are skipped.
    \item Never lose a good copy: two kinds of failure, retry (timeouts, overload) or give up (404,
      403 \dots); a failed re-fetch never replaces a good page.
    \item Idempotent: saving the same address twice changes nothing; ``confirmed'' only goes from
      false to true; the fetch date is never overwritten by a crawl. That is what makes a monthly,
      interruptible process safe.
  \end{itemize}}
\end{frame}
