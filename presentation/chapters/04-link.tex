    \hlP{Doge} is a slang term for ``\hlC{dog}'' that is primarily associated with pictures of

    photos may be captioned with interior \hlC{monologues} in \hlN{Comic Sans} font. The primary
    \hlC{meme} and \hlC{iconography} associated with \hlP{Doge} was the \hlN{Shiba Inu} named
    \hlN{Kabosu}, whose photos taken by her owner, Atsuko \hlC{Sato}, \dots
  \end{minipage}
  \vskip3mm
  {\normalsize\color{gmInk2}and every tag, whole:}\enspace
  \hlT{shiba inu}\enspace\hlT{kabosu}\enspace\hlT{dogecoin}\enspace\hlT{doges}\enspace\hlT{japanese}\enspace{\normalsize\dots}
  \vskip4mm
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=1cm, lg/.style={anchor=west, font=\small, text=gmInk2}]
    \node[lg] at (0,0) {\hlN{name} a named entity};
    \node[lg] at (4.1,0) {\hlP{name} a run of proper nouns};
    \node[lg] at (8.9,0) {\hlC{word} a noun phrase};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vskip3mm
  {\large\color{gmInk}\textbf{\NDogeMentions} names found on Doge's page --- by \textbf{spaCy}, on our own machine.}
  \note{
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The first step is natural-language processing: spaCy (the en\_core\_web\_sm model) reads
      the title and the About section and marks every span that could name something. Three ways
      of finding one: its named entities (Shiba Inus, Tumblr, Kabosu), runs of proper nouns
      (Doge), and noun phrases with every suffix of them (dog, monologues, meme). Each tag is
      tried whole: tags are lower-cased keywords, where there is no sentence to analyse.
    \item On purpose it finds too much: ``meme'', ``iconography'', ``Sato''. Recognition is
      recall-first; deciding what matters is a separate step, two stops from now.
    \item NER labels are a hint, never a filter: on the first real text the small model called
      4chan a number, Reddit a place and Shiba Inus an organisation. A filter on the label would
      have thrown three good links out of four.
    \item The title is special: Wikidata records Know Your Meme's own address for Doge (property
      P13484), so Doge's own item is certain --- score 1.0.
  \end{itemize}}
\end{frame}

  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=1cm,
      bx/.style={rounded corners=2mm, draw=gm@col@link, line width=1.2pt, fill=gmSurface, minimum width=3.05cm,
                 minimum height=1.95cm, align=center, text width=2.9cm, font=\small},
      go/.style={-{Stealth[length=2.8mm]}, line width=1.3pt, draw=gmBase, shorten <=1mm, shorten >=1mm}]
    \node[bx] (a) at (0,0) {{\normalsize\bfseries ``Shiba Inus''}\\[1mm]{\color{gmInk2}a name spaCy found}};
    \node[bx] (b) at (3.6,0) {{\normalsize\bfseries look it up}\\[1mm]{\color{gmInk2}\NLexEntities\ items, \NLexAliases\ names, from the \NDumpGB\ GB dump}};
    \node[bx] (c) at (7.2,0) {{\normalsize\bfseries rank the items}\\[1mm]{\color{gmInk2}popularity, context, label, type, KYM --- and the lead}};
    \node[bx, fill=gm@col@link, text=white] (d) at (10.8,0) {{\normalsize\bfseries Shiba Inu}\\[.5mm]dog breed, Q39315};
    \draw[go] (a) -- (b); \draw[go] (b) -- (c); \draw[go] (c) -- node[above, font=\small\bfseries, text=gmInk] {$\ge$ .45} (d);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vskip3mm
  \begin{columns}[c]
    \column{.5\textwidth}\centering
      \input{figures/generated/fig-link-bands}
    \column{.46\textwidth}
      {\large Where to draw the line?}\par\vskip2mm
      {\normalsize\color{gmInk2}\textbf{\color{gmInk}25 links read by hand per band} of the winner's score:
      below 0.45, half are wrong; above it, most are right.}
  \end{columns}
  \note{
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The lexicon is our own copy of Wikidata's names: the full dump of 14 September 2026
      (156 GB, 121.7 million lines), filtered to 17.3 million items with 22.6 million names ---
      items with an English name and a Wikipedia article, or a Know Your Meme identifier (many
      memes are on Wikidata and KYM and nowhere else), never a disambiguation page. 3.3 GB, built
      in about an hour.
    \item A name is looked up as written, then without a possessive, then with its plural folded
      (Shiba Inus $\to$ Shiba Inu); longer names first, and a linked name claims its characters.
    \item Every item that carries the name is ranked on five things: popularity (Wikipedia
      articles), context (its description shares words with the page), the label itself, the
      type agreeing with spaCy's label (a hint), and whether the item is itself on KYM. The
      winner's score adds how far it leads the runner-up: a unique exact match is strong evidence,
      a close race between two senses is weak.
    \item The line, 0.45, was drawn by reading: 403 random pages, 25 links per score band judged
      right or wrong by hand. 0.45--0.50 was as good as 0.50--0.55, so the line sits at 0.45:
      about 40\% more links, about 82\% of them the right item.
  \end{itemize}}
\end{frame}

    \node[ic] at (7.0,0) {\faRedo};            \node[tx] at (7.65,0) {\textbf{Same text, same links}\\[-.4mm]{\small\color{gmInk2}a dated dump, a fixed model}};
    \node[ic] at (0,-1.35) {\faBinoculars};    \node[tx] at (.65,-1.35) {\textbf{Reads more}\\[-.4mm]{\small\color{gmInk2}noun phrases, the title, every tag}};
    \node[ic] at (7.0,-1.35) {\faBalanceScale};\node[tx] at (7.65,-1.35) {\textbf{Keeps the evidence}\\[-.4mm]{\small\color{gmInk2}so the next step can judge}};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \note{
  \begin{itemize}
    \item IMKG sent the About text and the tags to DBpedia Spotlight (confidence 0.5), then mapped
      each DBpedia resource to Wikidata. A good choice for a one-off graph.
    \item For a graph rebuilt every month, four things decided it. One: we want Wikidata items,
      and the DBpedia-to-Wikidata mapping is incomplete --- going straight to Wikidata loses
      nothing on the way. Two: reproducibility --- the same text, the same dated dump and the same
      spaCy model always give the same links, so the dump's version is a stamp, and a page is
      re-linked exactly when its text, the dump or the model changes. Three: we read more ---
      noun phrases, the title (IMKG did not link titles), every tag whole. Four: every link keeps
      its score, method and features, which is what made curation possible.
    \item The suggestion to download all of Wikidata, instead of calling an API, came from the
      lab; the Wikidata API also refuses parallel anonymous calls (HTTP 429), and we link 24k
      pages at a time.
    \item The price: one long download and one long build per dump --- paid once, not per page.
  \end{itemize}}
\end{frame}

                                       3/k/keep/{a meme format}/\NCurFormat,
                                       4/k/keep/{also in the title}/\NCurTitleAgrees,

                                       6/d/drop/{About only: a body part, a measure}/\NCurDenyClass,

        \draw[gm@col@link, line width=1.2pt, -{Stealth[length=2.4mm]}] (0,8.55) -- (0,9.55);
        \node[anchor=west, font=\normalsize\bfseries, text=gmInk] at (.6,9.9) {the rest, \NCurJudged\ links: to the judge};
      \end{tikzpicture}
    \column{.4\textwidth}
      \begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=(current bounding box.north)]
        \node[fill=gm@col@link!10!gmSurface, rounded corners=2mm, inner sep=3mm, text width=4.9cm, align=left] {
          {\large\color{gm@col@link}\faGavel}\enspace{\normalsize\bfseries The judge: ministral-3:14b}\par\vskip1mm
          \small on the lab's GPUs, one call per page; each undecided item with its sentence. It answers a \textbf{role}:\par\vskip1mm
          {\color{gm@col@link}\bfseries keep}\enspace subject, source, format, platform\par
          {\color{gmMuted}\bfseries drop}\enspace incidental, wrong sense\par\vskip1mm
          {\color{gmInk2}Doge: \emph{monologue} kept (format); \emph{doges} (the doge of Venice), \emph{Donald Trump} dropped}};
      \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip0mm
  {\normalsize \NMentions\ names $\rightarrow$ \textbf{\NKept\ kept} (\NKeptPct\%), each with its reason.}
  \note{
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Recognising names is easy; deciding which matter to the meme is the hard part.
      Relevant (my definition, set before building): what the meme is about or shows, the work it
      comes from, the kind of meme it is, the platforms where it started or spread.
    \item Rules decide the clear cases, in this order; the count is links decided by that rule
      over the whole corpus. The title is always kept (it is the meme's name); deny lists drop
      words like ``popularity'' or ``the term''; platforms and meme formats are kept; a link both a
      tag and the text agree on is kept; About-only body parts, measures and generic words are
      dropped.
    \item The rest --- \NCurJudged\ links on \NCurFramesJudged\ pages --- go to an LLM judge: ministral-3:14b,
      running on the lab's GPU server. It is extractive: it sees each item's label, description and
      the words it was read from, and can only classify. It answers a role, which makes it commit
      to why. A keep based on the About alone must be confirmed by the same model asked in a
      second wording (\NCurConfirmOverturned\ overturned).
    \item Doge: monologue kept as a format; ``doges'' had been linked to the doge of Venice and
      ``Sato'' to a Puerto Rican slang word --- wrong senses, dropped; Donald Trump appears because
      of the DOGE department --- incidental, dropped.
    \item Nothing is deleted: every decision, with its reason, stays in the database; only kept
      links reach the graph.
  \end{itemize}}
\end{frame}

\gmgo{link-bakeoff}{How the rules were chosen}
\begin{frame}{How the rules and the judge were chosen}
  \begin{columns}[c]
    \column{.47\textwidth}
      \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=-1.42cm,
          nb/.style={circle, fill=gm@col@link, text=white, font=\normalsize\bfseries, minimum size=.62cm, inner sep=0pt},
          tx/.style={anchor=west, text width=5.6cm, font=\normalsize, text=gmInk}]
        \draw[gm@col@link!40!gmSurface, line width=1.4pt] (0,0) -- (0,3);
        \node[nb] at (0,0) {1}; \node[tx] at (.5,0) {\textbf{Measure the first run}: \FCurCommonNouns\% common nouns; ``series'' read as maths, \FCurSeries\ times};
        \node[nb] at (0,1) {2}; \node[tx] at (.5,1) {\textbf{Set the bar first}: kept $\ge$ \FCurBarKept\ relevant, dropped $\le$ \FCurBarDropped};
        \node[nb] at (0,2) {3}; \node[tx] at (.5,2) {\textbf{Bake-off}: 3 models, 4 prompts, 353 links labelled by hand};
        \node[nb] at (0,3) {4}; \node[tx] at (.5,3) {\textbf{Rules where judges failed}; the judge asked twice};
      \end{tikzpicture}
    \column{.5\textwidth}\centering
      \input{figures/generated/fig-link-bakeoff}\par\vskip1mm
      {\normalsize\color{gmInk2}chosen: kept \textbf{\color{gmInk}\FCurKept}, dropped \textbf{\color{gmInk}\FCurDropped}}
  \end{columns}
  \note{
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Measuring changed the design. The first full linking run (282,914 links) was measured
      before anything was built on it: 59\% of About links came from spans with no proper noun ---
      common nouns. Some hubs were plain wrong: KYM's stock phrase ``X is a series of \dots''
      linked to the mathematical series 5,531 times. That gave the sense list (``series'' is a
      series of works, ``game'' is a video game) and the curation stage.
    \item The bar was set before measuring: of kept links $\ge$ 0.85 relevant, of dropped $\le$ 0.15.
    \item The bake-off: 280 links labelled by hand, then 353, each setup projected over the corpus.
      Grey dots: the first prompts, three models (mistral-small, ministral, qwen3) --- none near the
      bar. What moved it was the rules: whole tags naming something were relevant 34 times in 39,
      and both judges dropped a third to half of them, not knowing a tag from the About --- so a
      rule keeps them; meme formats kept and generic About words dropped by list. Then asking
      ministral twice, in two wordings, beat asking a second model.
    \item The chosen setup projects to 0.875 kept, 0.126 lost. Caveat to say out loud: projected
      from labels the setup was chosen on; the holdout review is the measurement still to do.
    \item Two bigger models (llama3.3:70b, qwen3.6:35b) crashed the lab's GPU server and were
      dropped from the comparison.
  \end{itemize}}
\end{frame}
